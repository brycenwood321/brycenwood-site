Ten sections, in the order to do them

Each section is the move, then the details that matter. Sources are named where a number comes from a study.

1

Where each assistant gets its answer

Voice assistants and AI search mostly read listings and other people's pages, not just your website. A business can be number one on Google and invisible to Siri. We proved it: number one on Google and Yelp, and not in Apple Maps' top results at all, so Siri never said the name. The fix took 10 minutes.

Siri and Apple Maps: your Apple Business listing, with Yelp stars on the card. Google AI and Gemini: your Google Business Profile, then your website. Alexa: Yelp and Bing. Copilot: Bing Places. ChatGPT: a mix of Yelp, Bing, BBB, niche directories and your site. Claude and Perplexity: other sites about you, like directories and roundups. So you need all of them, with the exact same name, address, phone and hours.

2

Google Business Profile

Do this one first. It was 28.6% of every citation in Google's AI answers in BrightLocal's August 2026 study of 60,970 checks. The primary category is the number one map factor in Whitespark's expert survey. Search your main service, see what the top three use, and pick the closest real one.

Fill in everything: hours, holiday hours, services, products, photos, and a website link that goes to your lead or order page. Show your address only if customers really come there; Google requires permanent signage and staffed hours. Use your real name, never a keyword-stuffed one (it helps in surveys and gets profiles suspended). Ask reviewers to mention the specific service, because Google's AI quotes review text. Posting weekly is optional: Google lists no posting factor.

3

Apple, Bing, Yelp and the rest

Apple Business (free, business.apple.com) feeds Siri and Apple Maps. Pick the category your top competitors use, and add an Action button (Order, Book, Call) pointing at your conversion page. Bing Places imports straight from Google in about 5 minutes. Yelp is free and was in about 80% of ChatGPT's local answers in one 2026 study.

Also claim: Facebook Page (its address sets your Instagram map pin), Nextdoor, a free BBB profile, and the niche directories for your trade (Angi, HomeAdvisor, Houzz). Ask ChatGPT who it recommends for your service and note which sites it cites: get on those. Pick the closest category on every platform; each has its own list. One warning: registering Apple Business turns that email into a Managed Apple Account that cannot sign in to the App Store, so use a separate login. And write down which login owns each listing.

4

Reviews

Steady beats a burst: Sterling Sky found reviews this month mattered more than the total count. About 10 reviews gets you started where competition is thin. AI leans on them too: in one vendor study, businesses both ChatGPT and Perplexity recommended averaged 133 Google reviews, against 11 for businesses neither named.

Google's rules: no payment, discounts or freebies for reviews, and no asking only the happy customers (review gating). Ask everyone. A QR card with every job plus one text after is allowed and works. Get some on Yelp too, since Alexa and Apple Maps read it. No source backs a monthly target like "8 to 15 a month", so ignore those.

5

Website pages

Google ranks pages, not sites. Build one page per service and one page per city, each with its own title, local details and a short FAQ. Our first city pages were 88% to 92% identical; we rewrote them under about 40% alike so they were not doorway pages.

Add a page for each real buyer question: cost, "us vs the big national brand" (date every competitor price), who it is for, how it works. AI engines quote pages titled like the question. Keep one answer per fact on every page (we found two different cutoff times on two pages, and AI repeats contradictions). Only claim what you can prove. Put a tap-to-call number above the fold and a short form that reaches a human in under 2 minutes, and test a real submission end to end. Link every new page from the footer the same day.

6

Website technical setup

Put your facts in the raw HTML. ChatGPT's, Claude's and Perplexity's crawlers do not run JavaScript, so prices or hours that load by script are a blank page to them. Check with curl -s yoursite.com | grep "price".

Every page: unique title under 60 characters, description 50 to 160, one h1, a canonical tag. robots.txt blocks nothing public and names the AI crawlers (copy box below). A sitemap with real dates, submitted to Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools. LocalBusiness schema whose name matches your Google listing exactly, with sameAs links to your Google, Apple, Yelp and Facebook pages so every engine sees one business (copy box below). Redirect any staging copy to the real domain. Unknown pages return a real 404.

7

How each AI engine picks a business

Studies disagree, so cover every source instead of betting on one. One week, on the same 15 buyer questions, Gemini named a business 14 times and Claude only 9. Gemini searches Google, where the business's own pages rank. Claude finds local businesses mostly through other people's sites.

Fixing your own site moves Gemini. Mentions on other sites move Claude and ChatGPT. The top AI factor in Whitespark's survey is being on expert "best of" lists. Being known by name is not the same as being recommended: test both kinds of question. ChatGPT now has free business listings (chatgpt.com/business-hub). Read the terms first: OpenAI says claiming "does not guarantee higher placement", and a published profile cannot be deleted.

8

Backlinks and mentions

Every site that links to you or mentions you is a vote that you are real. Google uses links to decide who to trust, and AI engines like Claude and ChatGPT find local businesses mostly through other people's pages. For AI answers, Whitespark puts it simply: mentions are the new link. This is how you get named by the engines that do not read your own site.

Who actually links to local businesses: local roundup articles ("best plumbers in [city]"), local TV segments (the show page usually has a pitch form), event and team sponsor pages, government help guides that list local vendors, local "best of" contests, and the directories already ranking for your terms. How to pitch: find a page that already links to businesses like yours, use the contact published on their own site (generic info@ addresses often bounce), keep it short, and offer something real for their readers: a local story, a useful price guide, a quote for a journalist. Follow up at about day 10, because most replies come from the second email. Keep a simple list of who you pitched and when. Free journalist-request services (Source of Sources, Qwoted, Featured) are worth a weekly look. Never trade services for links, swap links, or buy link packages or Reddit mentions.

9

Measuring it honestly

AI answers change run to run: in one study Gemini's repeat answers shared sources only 26% to 46% of the time. So one check means nothing. Write 10 to 20 buyer questions, freeze them, and run each 3 times per engine, logged out, in a private window, with your location set. Count a mention only if it shows up in 2 of 3.

Never test from your own logged-in account: the AI remembers you (one of our runs said "This is your own shop, so I'm listing it first"). Track named vs top pick separately, since being named fifth is not being recommended. Set up Google Search Console on day one and check it before building more: pages with views and few clicks need better titles before you add new pages. Bing Webmaster Tools counts your citations in Copilot answers.

10

Myths to ignore

"One file will make AI recommend you." Google says there are no extra technical requirements for its AI answers. An llms.txt or schema is a cheap, good habit that keeps your facts clean, but it works as part of the whole stack (listings, reviews, pages, mentions), not on its own. "Claiming your ChatGPT profile boosts ranking." OpenAI says it does not. "Foursquare feeds 70% of ChatGPT." Measured at 0% in a direct test.

Also skip: posting weekly "because active businesses rank higher", keywords in your business name, "add 1,000 to 2,000 words", monthly review quotas, and any stat with no source. Never do: fake or rented Google profiles (Google sued operators over 350+ fake ones), virtual-office addresses, review gating, paid reviews, link swaps. The real work is boring: same details everywhere, steady reviews, one clear page per question.

The AI visibility check

I live in [YOUR TOWN, STATE]. I need [WHAT YOU DO, in the words a customer would use, like "a plumber for a leaking water heater"].

Search the web and answer the way you normally would. Name the specific local businesses you would recommend, best first, and say in one line why you picked each one.

Then list every website you used to decide, one per line.

The review ask text

Hey [FIRST NAME], thanks again for having us out for [THE JOB]. If you have 30 seconds, an honest Google review helps a small business like ours more than anything. Mentioning what we did ([THE JOB]) helps the next person find us.

[YOUR GOOGLE REVIEW LINK]

Thank you!
