The Plumbing Problem

Plumbing is a business built on urgency. When a pipe bursts, nobody waits. They call the first company that answers. And if that is not you, it is your competitor.

Here is the reality of running a plumbing company. The phone rings at 10 PM on a Saturday because someone's water heater just flooded their basement. It goes to voicemail. Your tech checks the message on Monday morning. By then, the customer has already called three other companies and hired the one that picked up.

During business hours, it is not much better. Your plumber is under a sink at 2 PM when three calls come in. Your office manager is processing an invoice. The calls go to voicemail. Two of those three callers will not leave a message -- they will just call the next plumber in the search results. That is revenue you will never even know you lost.

Then there is the follow-up problem. You replaced a water heater for a customer two years ago. That customer's sewer line probably needs an inspection. Their water softener needs a filter change. Their outdoor spigots need winterization. But nobody in your company is tracking that. Nobody is reaching out. And when that customer does need a plumber again, they might not remember your name -- but they will remember the one who showed up in their inbox last week with a helpful reminder.

Plumbing is not a one-time transaction business. It is a relationship business. And relationships require follow-up. The companies that follow up systematically build recurring revenue streams that smooth out the feast-or-famine cycle. The ones that do not are forever dependent on the next phone call from a stranger.

Built for How Plumbing Actually Works

I have worked with plumbing companies as customers for years. These systems are designed around the specific realities of the plumbing business.

The Phone is Everything -- And You Are Missing Calls

In plumbing, the phone is the front door. When a customer has a leak, a clog, or a broken water heater, they are calling someone right now. Not tomorrow. Not after they research five companies. Right now. Studies show that 80% of callers who reach voicemail will not leave a message -- they will just call the next number. The emergency routing system ensures that every call gets a response within minutes, 24 hours a day. For the calls that come in at 3 AM, the customer gets an immediate confirmation and an ETA, not a voicemail box.

Your Customer List is a Gold Mine You Are Not Mining

A plumbing company with 5 years of history has hundreds -- sometimes thousands -- of past customers. Each one of those customers has equipment that ages, pipes that corrode, and drains that slow down. The maintenance reminder system works through your existing customer base systematically. Water heater installed 8 years ago? That customer gets a friendly "your water heater is approaching end-of-life" message with an inspection offer. Sewer line scoped 3 years ago? Time for another look. The revenue from these reminders is high-margin, predictable, and comes from customers who already trust you.

Emergency Plumbing Searches Happen at the Worst Times

Nobody Googles "emergency plumber" at 10 AM on a Tuesday. They Google it at 11 PM on a Sunday when their toilet is overflowing. Your website needs to work as a conversion tool at those exact moments. The build includes a site optimized for emergency searches with one-tap calling, instant booking forms, and clear pricing information. When a panicked homeowner lands on your site at midnight, the path from "I need help" to "help is on the way" should be under 30 seconds.

Customer Lifetime Value is the Real Metric

The average plumbing customer is not worth $300 for one drain cleaning. They are worth $5,000-$15,000 over 10 years when you account for water heater replacements, sewer repairs, fixture upgrades, and annual maintenance. The automation system tracks and optimizes for lifetime value, not single-transaction revenue. Every touchpoint -- from the first emergency call to the five-year water heater check-in -- is designed to keep your company top-of-mind and top-of-trust.

Common Questions

How does the emergency call routing work after hours? +

The system captures every after-hours call and immediately sends the customer a confirmation that their request was received. It categorizes the urgency -- a burst pipe gets routed to the on-call plumber within 60 seconds, while a dripping faucet gets scheduled for the next available slot. The on-call tech receives the customer details, location, and problem description via text. The customer receives an ETA. Nobody has to check voicemail in the morning. Nobody falls through the cracks. For true emergencies, the response time from customer call to tech notification is under two minutes.

Can this integrate with my existing dispatch and invoicing software? +

Yes. Every build integrates with your current tools. If you are on ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, Jobber, FieldEdge, or any other field service platform, the automation layer sits on top of what you already use. If you are still running on spreadsheets and paper invoices, I will set up a proper system as part of the build. The goal is always to enhance your existing workflow, not force you into something unfamiliar.

How do maintenance reminders increase revenue? +

Most plumbing companies service a customer once and never contact them again unless the customer calls back. The maintenance reminder system automatically reaches out to past customers at strategic intervals -- water heater flush reminders, sewer line inspection schedules, seasonal pipe winterization alerts, and water softener maintenance. These are legitimate service needs that customers appreciate being reminded about. A typical plumbing company has hundreds of past customers who would book a maintenance visit if someone simply reminded them. At an average ticket of $200-$400 per maintenance call, even a 10% booking rate on reminder campaigns generates significant recurring revenue from customers you have already earned.

What does this cost compared to hiring a dispatcher? +

A full-time dispatcher costs $3,000-$4,500 per month in salary, plus benefits, plus the management overhead of training and supervising another employee. The custom build is a one-time investment of $3,500 at the founder rate. The systems handle call routing, scheduling, follow-ups, reminders, and review requests -- the core tasks that consume a dispatcher's day. They work 24/7, never call in sick, and never forget to follow up. For plumbing companies with 3-10 trucks, the automation typically handles the workload equivalent of one to two office staff.
