The Ecommerce Problem

Your store works. Orders are coming in. But you can feel the revenue you're leaving on the table -- in carts that never converted, customers who never came back, and ads you can't tell are profitable.

You started the brand because you saw a gap in the market or you couldn't stop thinking about a product idea. The product is dialed. The brand looks great. The site converts well enough. But the work behind the storefront keeps growing, and most of it is invisible to customers.

Carts get abandoned at a 70%+ rate -- that's normal for ecommerce, but the recovery sequence you set up six months ago is generic and tired. Customers complete one order and never come back because nothing reminds them to. The 200 reviews you should have are 28 reviews because asking for them is awkward and your post-purchase email gets buried. Inventory levels live in three places that never agree. Returns and refunds eat hours every week. Ad performance lives in three dashboards that each tell a different story about ROAS.

You know the customer journey can be tighter. You know the email flows can do more. You know the reviews and the post-purchase experience and the win-back campaigns can compound into real revenue. But every time you sit down to fix one of them, two new fires need attention and you end up answering DMs about a missing order.

This is what most ecommerce operators run into around $25-100K/month. The store works. The operations don't scale. Hiring a marketing agency feels expensive and slow. Hiring an in-house developer is a $100K+ commitment. The off-the-shelf tools each solve one slice and leave the integration to you. Meanwhile, the brand that figures out the operating system pulls away.

The Universal Pattern

Every ecommerce brand that breaks past the operating ceiling does the same five things. The automation makes all five happen without doubling your team.

1. Recover Every Cart You Can

Most stores accept a 70%+ cart abandonment rate as just how it is. It isn't. A well-tuned recovery sequence -- email at 1 hour, SMS at 24 hours, email at 72 hours, retargeting trigger throughout -- recovers 10-15% of those carts. On a store doing $50K/month, that's $5-7K of pure recovered revenue every month with no new ads. The math compounds. This is the single highest-ROI automation in ecommerce, and most brands ship a generic three-email Klaviyo template and call it done.

2. Turn First-Time Buyers Into Repeat Customers

The first purchase is the hardest one you'll ever earn. Then most brands fumble it. A real post-purchase journey -- thank-you, product education, review request, replenishment reminder, cross-sell -- can lift 60-day repurchase rate from 8% to 18-22%. That's the difference between a brand that needs to find new customers every month and a brand that has a flywheel.

3. Compound Your Social Proof

Reviews are the deciding factor for most ecommerce purchases. A brand with 1,200 reviews at 4.7 stars wins against a brand with 47 reviews and a better product. Automated review collection -- timed to product arrival, segmented by product, with photo/video prompts -- builds that gap on autopilot. Twelve months of consistent collection separates you from the field entirely.

4. Know Your Real Numbers

Most ecommerce operators are flying blind on the metrics that actually matter. Not revenue -- contribution margin. Not ROAS -- true blended CAC. Not "good month / bad month" -- LTV by acquisition channel. The dashboard surfaces all of it in real time. When you can see that Meta is generating $4.20 LTV per dollar and TikTok is generating $1.80, you reallocate. When you can see that Tuesday's email did 3x what Thursday's did, you adjust. Data makes you smarter. Automation makes the data show up.

5. Build a Brand the Operating System Can Carry

The brands that scale past $1M and $10M and $50M aren't doing fundamentally different marketing than the brands stuck at $500K. They're running the same plays with operational discipline that doesn't break as orders compound. The automation is what lets a team of three feel like a team of fifteen. Your brand voice, your customer journey, your retention plays -- all running 24/7 whether you're at the warehouse or asleep.

Common Questions

Which ecommerce platforms do you build on? +

Shopify, WooCommerce, BigCommerce, and custom stacks. The automation layer sits alongside your store, not inside it -- so I'm not limited by what your platform allows. Cart events, order data, customer profiles, and inventory all flow through Claude Code-built systems that talk to your store via API. If you can export data and webhook events from your platform, I can build automation around it.

How is this different from Klaviyo, Omnisend, or other ecommerce email tools? +

Off-the-shelf email tools give you templated flows. You still have to write every email, build every segment, configure every trigger, and figure out how it all connects to your ads, your reviews, your inventory, and your support. I build the full operating system. The emails are one layer. The customer journey logic, the inventory automation, the review collection, the ad performance dashboard, and the LTV tracking are what actually compound revenue. The tools become components inside a system that's tuned for your specific brand.

Do I need a certain revenue level to make this worth it? +

If you're doing $20K+/month and spending on ads, the ROI is fast -- cart recovery alone typically pays for the build inside 60 days. Below $20K/month, the better starting point is the community ($97/mo) where you can build the foundational flows yourself with the exact prompts and templates I use. The custom build is for brands that already have product-market fit and are losing revenue to operational gaps, not for brands still figuring out what to sell.

What's the highest-ROI automation for an ecommerce store? +

Cart and checkout recovery, by a wide margin. Most stores lose 60-80% of their carts. A well-tuned recovery sequence -- email plus SMS plus retargeting trigger -- typically recovers 10-15% of abandoned carts and lifts overall revenue 8-12% without adding any new traffic. After cart recovery, the next highest-ROI builds are post-purchase upsell sequences, automated review collection, and segmented win-back campaigns for customers who haven't ordered in 90+ days. The combined lift for a store running all four is usually 20-30% on existing traffic.
